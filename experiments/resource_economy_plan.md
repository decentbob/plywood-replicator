# P2: material economy before polygon implementation

2026-09-26; baseline 19cd4e6. Analytical assay only; no simulation rules change.
Question: can a fixed small palette trade additional common material for less
scarce material per independently renewing fragment, with no resource-free
small competitor and a contact-local renewal path? Sections 40 and 61–62
motivate checking material accounting and closure before adding mechanisms.

## Scope fixed before enumeration

1. Use the published reversible contact-table candidate numbered 670873 in
   Schulman & Winfree (2011), figure 6, as a single analytical candidate. Decode
   its twenty output bits using figure 3's f1..f6 order. State explicitly any
   ambiguity in the textual extraction and cross-check the claimed period
   structure before relying on the mapping. No broad table search.
2. Describe twelve immutable polygon edge-label types, not a programmable block
   or a runtime cellular-automaton interpreter. A mathematical sweep through
   labels is an offline accounting device, never a proposed global action.
   No lattice dynamics, geometry replacement, hidden counter, completed-row
   detector, chosen fragmentation event or material replenishment in the world.
3. Enumerate every binary boundary pattern for interior widths n=1..8, including
   cycles and any transients. Examine each of the four possible scarce boundary
   types. Retain every cycle, including zero-scarce competitors. Use n=1,2,3
   for a three-organization material comparison if the mapping is consistent.
4. Distinguish scarce fraction, demand per growth column, demand per full repeat,
   and demand per viable independent child. A fixed two-column fragment is only
   a provisional information unit; check its exposed contacts and the actual
   ability to restart attachment. A full-period seed is not a free input.
5. Calculate finite-stock bounds, returned versus trapped material, and required
   contact formation/break operations. No constant-concentration growth-time
   estimate is a closed-pool population prediction. Include the one-scarce-cap
   lengthening control, where dilution of scarce fraction saves no part per child.

## Candidate block contract and gate

Candidate contact formation tests fixed port labels and polygon contact geometry.
Retention may read only the block's own incident bonds (weak one-contact versus
strong multiple-contact retention); a loss request affects an incident bond.
Zero new mutable state or relayed signal is preferred. Generic contact labels
must account for every local input/output case. No partner identity, sequence,
width, global orientation, phase number or completed-fragment read is allowed.

Before any physical assay, require all three: a concrete material trade-off;
no zero-scarce periodic winner in the examined small candidates; and an explicit
sequence of individual contact operations that produces two viable fragments
without a global cleavage or an externally supplied activation pattern. Test
forward and reverse exposed ends. A two-contact-only attachment rule must not
silently suppress the one-contact intermediates required in polygon motion.

Stop or mark the candidate incomplete if geometry/renewal remains unresolved,
if the table mapping is unverified, if apparent savings vanish per child, or
if a simpler zero-scarce competitor defeats the rationale. Do not add states
to bypass the failed gate or run population simulations. A conditional algebraic
trade-off alone is not reproductive closure or evolved complexity.
