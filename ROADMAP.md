## P0 — local rules, with targeted physics validation

**User-approved distinction (2026-09-26):** strictly local simple reaction rules and emergent
mechanics are fundamental; strict locality of every numerical physics operation is not.
Keep body jostling for fast exploration. It supplies aggregate random motion, not copying
instructions, but it omits relative random kicks inside a bonded assembly. Do not claim it
is universally equivalent to individual kicks or automatically more accurate than them.

1. For rules being introduced or investigated, review their read/write interface. Keep immutable
   side identity, previous-pass relayed signals and contact geometry separate from observer
   metadata. No whole-organism action, ancestor reader or global completion/stall predicate.
   Audit live derived-state dependencies when relevant; avoid a broad refactor without a defect.
2. Explore with the current physics and matched controls. For a promising mechanical claim,
   compare the effect with individual kicks and sufficient solver passes, preserving the
   experimental contrast in each setting. Measure useful output and the proposed physical
   mechanism, not identical trajectories or identical births per step.
3. Check solver sensitivity before attributing differences to the jostling scheme. Section 49
   recovered the qualitative fit benefit at 16 individual-kick passes in two seeds; this is
   useful prior evidence, not a universal convergence setting. Section 47's onset effect also
   differed between schemes. Neither path is an unquestionable physical ground truth.
4. If the claimed mechanism survives, proceed with the faster setting and report quantitative
   sensitivity. If it disappears or reverses, isolate why and limit the claim before scaling.
   Change the physics only when that discrepancy matters to the research question. A suspected
   mechanically suppressed effect can also justify a small individual-kick diagnostic.

**Gate for a strong mechanical claim:** a matched causal effect with adequate solver resolution
and a documented sensitivity check, or an explicitly limited conclusion under the tested motion
model. This does not require replaying all historical results, disabling every optional physical
force, or migrating defaults. Rule/observer locality checks remain mandatory for relevant changes.

## P1 — ecology that can generate its own next problem

**Hypothesis:** inherited differences in product binding/delivery create frequency-dependent
reproductive consequences, so a common strategy changes which variants are useful next.
This is stronger goal alignment than assigning one extra fixed reward to a fourth motif.

**First experiment:** establish genuine recipient dependence in an already viable shared-product
world. Section 44 warns that capped non-producers can reproduce at the tested bare rate; section
46 rejects flexibility's recipient benefit. Those settings do not establish the required dependence.
Use existing rules and measured viable parameters, with matched initial material and physics.
Compare binding enabled/disabled and a production-capable/inactive source control that retains
physical material. Report absolute recipient-parent reproduction, source reproduction, delivered
occupancy and unfinished inventory. If an ablation changes other behavior, state that limitation.

**Promote only if:** removing the interaction reproducibly reduces recipient reproduction and
the donor population can renew itself. A recipient share increase caused by donor collapse fails.
Before a long evolution run, show a trade-off or frequency dependence in a small matched competition
at rare and common starting frequencies, preserving overall density and letter inventory as far
as possible. Larger populations are earned by an identified effect susceptible to drift.

**Next if earned:** hold the rule table fixed, enable existing physical mutation channels and
ask whether persistent new interaction patterns arise. Recover evolved variants for common
environment competitions and contact ablations, using prepared reruns as measurements only.
Observe causal dependency networks as well as sequence length. A key changing once is adaptation;
cycling keys without added capability is not cumulative complexity.

**Stop:** no recipient dependence, renewed host collapse, or identity-aware recognition needed
to rescue the assay. No same-maker exclusion, whole-key matcher, artificial fitness score or
automatic replenishment. Review [the literature update](LITERATURE.md#research-directions-rechecked-2026-09-26)
for why spatial models motivate this hypothesis without supplying compliant rules.

**2026-09-26 screen outcome (61):** the existing section-36 PY setting passes its
predeclared two-seed gate. Both production and binding ablations lower absolute
recipient-parent output; producers still reproduce. Detached exact recipient
output is 15/18 versus 0/1 without binding and 2/1 without production. The
production control retains all blocks and binding compatibility using an absent
translation-start type; it also changes back exposure and construction contacts.
This does not reopen the capped flexibility hypothesis (46).

**2026-09-26 confirmation outcome (62):** unchanged four arms, 50k, seeds
105–108, primary window (10k,50k]. Recipient binding contrasts are +16/-1/+22/+4;
production contrasts +21/+3/+21/+4. Seeds 105/107 pass; 106 loses the binding
benefit, and 108 misses the minimum effect despite positive direction. All four
retain producer reproduction and nonzero recipient occupancy. Detached exact
output also reverses the binding contrast in 106. Mean benefits are positive,
but the predeclared all-four gate fails. No retrospective threshold change.

**Disposition:** park this setting for frequency competition. No rate tuning,
extra confirmation seeds, longer runs or new states. Low occupancy in seed 106
is an observation, not an isolated causal obstruction that reopens the branch.
P2 is the next main question; P4 remains an independent fallback. The two assays
strengthen evidence for autonomous interaction but do not establish reliable
recipient benefit by the fixed criterion, full reproductive closure or novelty.

## P2 — extra organization that economizes scarce material

**Deep-dive question:** can a small fixed palette have several heritable assemblies where more
organization reduces consumption of a scarce part enough to repay assembly and maintenance cost?
Schulman–Winfree is a useful example, with substantial assumptions to remove. Ordinary stacks
in section 40 only repeat rows and hoard letters; they do not implement the paper's resource-use logic.

**First deliverable (before implementation):** a short derivation and candidate block-level table.
For at least two, preferably three assemblies of increasing organization, calculate or enumerate
material usage and required local contacts per successful renewal. Search for simpler competitors
that use no scarce part at all. Explicitly account for finite stock and recycling; an early growth
advantage followed by irreversible sequestration is insufficient.

Each proposed transition must be a one-block contact operation; weak single-contact retention
and stronger multiple-contact retention are candidates. Do not import a grid, a hidden integer
counter, an entire cellular-automaton interpreter, a completed-row detector, crystal-level
scission probability or unlimited monomer reservoir. Assembly-level computation emerging from
small local parts is admissible only if the parts themselves remain simple and general.

**Gate:** a concrete trade-off under one fixed rule table, no trivial resource-free winner in
the examined small competitors, and a plausible contact-local renewal path. Then test physical
growth and naturally produced viable fragments, followed by inherited variation and competition.
Track functional organization separately from width; the latter is only a paper-specific proxy.

**Stop:** the argument depends on chemostatting, scripted dilution/scission, or increasingly
special-case state logic. No new engine and no large crystal population before the derivation
and a small physical cycle work. Passing the gate would materially raise this route's priority.

## P3 — an inherited mechanical part must earn its keep

**Starting evidence:** complementary shapes restore curved copying (49); shape holds fuel (50);
fully active descendants still stall (51). Braces straighten but do not increase sustained yield
(47); release and handoff can work in selected fixtures without a general advantage (56–60).

Choose **one** of these hypotheses, justified by a measured bottleneck:

- **Geometric specificity:** at fixed composition, inherited arrangements create different
  contact selectivity or fuel access. First demonstrate a reproductive consequence under matched
  physics, followed by the P0 sensitivity check, not another uptake-only race. Section 41's drift and section 50's missing second
  generation must be addressed explicitly. Complementary pairing alone is not evolved division of labor.
- **Steric work from attachment:** existing contacts between an arc/ribbon and attached bulky
  parts cause bending or useful access, without prescribing the desired bend. Compare no-binding
  and smaller-bound-part controls with a common solver. Size differences can change diffusion and
  occupancy, so measure them instead of attributing everything to force. This is distinct from
  the failed straight brace and from changing a rest shape by decree.

**First gate:** a causal physical effect improves useful output over the appropriate simpler
control. **Second gate:** parts are assembled, reach their sites, act and are renewed without
prepared placement; offspring repeat the cycle with conserved material. Account for the cost of
building and occupying the support. Only then test inheritance and selection.

**Stop:** more capture, bending or retained contacts with no net reproductive effect. Do not
make a polymerase/walker simply because it can be implemented as many local states. Introduce
one only if a named physical operation cannot be supplied more simply and the cost is measurable.

## P4 — distinguish heredity from recurring shapes in random chemistry

Section 32 lists candidates 55, 57, 4, 15, 1 and 54. Confirm the archived table definitions and
initial physics before selecting a small subset; preserve the positive `copyTable` control.
Do not finish the remaining 69-table search before improving the discrimination assay.

**First assay:** equal-material prepared and unseeded baths plus a disrupted-contact control.
All use fixed tables, conserved blocks, recorded initial geometry and independent bath seeds.
Ask whether a seed causes new disjoint material to acquire and propagate its specific organization.
Then compare at least two distinguishable viable starting variants under the same table: do
their descendants preserve the variant, or converge to the same ordinary self-assembled shape?

Count descendants from physical event/member histories; resolve candidate graph-hash matches
with exact side-labelled structure comparisons. Use shape signatures that do not mistake
transient internal-state phases for new species. Transplanting consumes free material and changes
geometry: no-seed counts plus the number transplanted alone are not an adequate control.

**Gate:** at least one candidate shows autonomous, variant-specific propagation beyond founder
material and descendant turnover. Only then test differential persistence or a new bounded search.
**Stop:** extra founders merely catalyze one unique equilibrium shape, counts are all founder
fragments, or no candidate exceeds controls. Table optimization by the researcher remains outer
design search; it must never be reported as the simulated population evolving its rules.

## P5 — coupled replication modes, only after the parts work

Potential route: one assembly retains/delivers a resource that another assembly needs, while
the second supplies useful contacts or structural material to the first. This could select
co-inheritance or division of labor without naming either in the dynamics. A strand merely
nucleating a stack does not demonstrate this.

Before coupling anything, write a material/contact diagram and predict outcomes with each
interaction disabled. Both modes must renew themselves from the fixed pool, and the coupled
system must retain its benefit after loss of the originally prepared helpers. Reciprocal benefit
must be observed, not enforced by a rule testing partner species or complete assemblies.
First candidates should reuse P1/P3 successes, not add a second speculative subsystem.

## C1 — optional closure of contact handoff

Apply the **unchanged** section-60 handshake to the original selected two-prefix obstruction
(56), with unchanged chemistry, SEEK and request/wait controls. Predeclare a common finite
horizon and compare useful exact output, original-member intact reuse and actual support
acquisition. Do not run a fresh-seed sweep or tune the hazard. Beat waiting by the specified
useful outcome; suppression of all completion does not count.

If the operation has no useful advantage there, mark the branch parked and preserve its operation
library. If it succeeds, it earns a bounded generalization test, not immediate core promotion or
an evolved-machine claim. Historical fixtures retain their recorded physics; a promising
mechanical result needs the P0 sensitivity check before a strong general claim.
